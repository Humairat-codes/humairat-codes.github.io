In computer networks, communication is classified by **how many receivers** a sender is targeting.

## Overview

| Type | Pattern | Delivered to | Typical scope |
|------|---------|--------------|---------------|
| Unicast | 1 : 1 | One specific receiver | Internet-wide |
| Broadcast | 1 : all | Every device on the segment | Local network (LAN) |
| Multicast | 1 : many | Members of a joined group | LAN / WAN |
| Anycast | 1 : nearest | The closest of several servers | Internet-wide |

---

## Unicast

- **What it is:** Direct, one-to-one communication between a single sender and a single receiver.
- **How it works:** Packets are addressed to one specific destination IP address. For example, if 50 people stream a movie from Netflix, the server opens 50 separate unicast streams, each carrying its own unique packets.
- **Use cases:** Most everyday internet traffic, such as browsing websites, checking email, downloading files, and SSH connections.

---

## Broadcast

- **What it is:** Transmission from one source to **every device** on a local network segment (LAN).
- **How it works:** The sender uses a special broadcast address (like `255.255.255.255`). Every device receives and processes the packet, whether it wants it or not. Routers normally block broadcasts from crossing into other networks, which prevents congestion (*broadcast storms*).
- **Use cases:**
  - ARP (Address Resolution Protocol) looking up a MAC address
  - DHCP discovery messages when a device first connects to Wi-Fi

---

## Multicast

- **What it is:** Transmission from one source to a **specific group** of interested receivers.
- **How it works:** Instead of flooding the whole network (broadcast) or sending a separate copy to everyone (unicast), the sender transmits a single stream to a special multicast IP address. Devices must explicitly **join** the group to receive the traffic. Switches and routers copy the data only down paths where receivers are listening.
- **Use cases:** IPTV, live video streaming, online gaming lobbies, and video conferencing, where one feed goes out to multiple participants.

---

## Anycast

- **What it is:** Data from one sender can go to **any one of several** destination nodes, and the network automatically routes it to the closest or best one.
- **How it works:** Multiple servers around the world share the exact same IP address. Using routing protocols like BGP, routers deliver each request to the node with the shortest path or lowest latency.
- **Use cases:** Content Delivery Networks (CDNs like Cloudflare or Google) and DNS root servers, so you always query the nearest server for fast responses.
