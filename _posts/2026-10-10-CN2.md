**Subnetting** is the process of dividing a network into smaller networks.

Example: `N1` → `S1`, `S2`, `S3`, `S4`

## Advantages

- Maintenance and administration become easier
- Higher security

## Disadvantages

- **It adds a step to identification.** Normally you identify *Network → Host → Port*. With subnetting you identify *Network → Subnet → Host → Port*.
- **Some IP addresses are wasted.** Each subnet loses addresses that could otherwise have been given to hosts.
- Extra routers, switches, etc. are required.
- A network admin is needed to manage the subnets.

---

## How to make a subnet

Subnets are made by **borrowing bits from the Host ID**.

Say a network has a NID of 3 bits. That gives 2³ = **8 networks**. For subnetting you want *more* networks, so you take some bits from the host part and add them to the network part.

If you borrow **1 bit** from the host, the NID becomes 4 bits, which gives 2⁴ = **16 networks**. In general, borrowing 1 bit doubles the number of networks (8 × 2 = 16).

## Example: 4 subnets from 200.200.200.0

Say you have the network `200.200.200.0` and you want **4 subnets**.

1. Borrow **2 bits** from the host part of the last octet. These become the **subnet ID**: `00`, `01`, `10`, `11`.
2. The remaining **6 bits** of the last octet are the **host bits**.
3. Hosts per subnet = 2⁶ − 2 = **62 hosts/subnet** (the all-0's and all-1's host addresses are reserved).
4. The new subnet mask is `255.255.255.192`, since the last octet is `11000000`.

| Subnet | Subnet ID bits | Network address | Usable hosts | Broadcast address |
|--------|----------------|-----------------|--------------|-------------------|
| S1 | `00` | `200.200.200.0` | `.1` – `.62` | `200.200.200.63` |
| S2 | `01` | `200.200.200.64` | `.65` – `.126` | `200.200.200.127` |
| S3 | `10` | `200.200.200.128` | `.129` – `.190` | `200.200.200.191` |
| S4 | `11` | `200.200.200.192` | `.193` – `.254` | `200.200.200.255` |

### Reading an address in binary

Look at the last octet: `[subnet ID: 2 bits][host ID: 6 bits]`

- `200.200.200.00000001` is the **1st host of the 1st subnet**.
- **Question:** What is the IP address of the 3rd host in the 2nd subnet?
  - Subnet = `01`, host = `000011`
  - **Answer:** `200.200.200.01000011` (which is `200.200.200.67`)

## Subnet address and Direct Broadcast Address (DBA)

| Subnet | Subnet address | DBA |
|--------|----------------|-----|
| `00` | `200.200.200.0` | `200.200.200.63` |
| `01` | `200.200.200.64` | `200.200.200.127` |
| `10` | `200.200.200.128` | `200.200.200.191` |
| `11` | `200.200.200.192` | `200.200.200.255` |

### Did you notice something?

- The subnet address of the **first** subnet (`200.200.200.0`) is the same as the address of the **entire network**.
- The DBA of the **last** subnet (`200.200.200.255`) is the same as the DBA of the **entire network**.

**So the real question is:** how do we know whether a broadcast is meant for the *entire network* or for just *one subnet*? External networks have no idea whether subnetting has been done.

### Answer

- **From outside, subnetting is invisible.** An external sender only knows the classful network `200.200.200.0` (Class C, mask `255.255.255.0`). A packet sent to `200.200.200.255` is, to them, simply the direct broadcast of the whole network.
- **Inside, the router knows the subnet mask.** The network's own router uses the mask (`255.255.255.192`) to split addresses into subnet ID and host ID, and it handles the packet accordingly.
- **The address itself is ambiguous.** `200.200.200.255` could mean "all of S4" or "all subnets". This is why the original classful rules (RFC 950) **forbade using the all-0's and all-1's subnets** (`00` and `11` here), leaving only 2 usable subnets out of 4.
- **Modern networks avoid the problem.** With CIDR and classless addressing, the all-0's and all-1's subnets are allowed, and the mask is always carried along with the address. Also, most routers today **block direct broadcasts** from outside for security reasons.

> **Takeaway:** Without the subnet mask, an address alone can't tell you whether it refers to a subnet or to the whole network. The mask resolves it, and outsiders don't have it.
